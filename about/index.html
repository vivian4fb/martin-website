Biography

A life spent with light

Born in London in 1958, Martin Richardson has spent his working life on the science and art of holography.

His doctorate at the Royal College of Art — the first PhD awarded in holography — pursued optical reduction, using complex optical systems and silver-halide recording to enlarge or reduce the recorded human form.

Honours followed: the Millennium Fellowship, sponsored by the UK Government Commission, and in 2009 the Royal Photographic Society’s Saxby Medal for his contribution to holographic imaging and its science.

At De Montfort University, Leicester, he led the Modern Holography Group and mentored students who became pioneers of the field. He now holds the title of Emeritus Professor, and his current work brings advanced optics together with AI to produce holographic displays of convincing three-dimensional presence.

He has recorded holographic portraits of Martin Scorsese, Alan Parker, Sir Peter Blake, Will Self, Auberon Waugh and David Bowie.
